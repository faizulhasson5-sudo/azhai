Google Algorithm Update History: 2000–2026

Last updated: July 2026 · Comprehensive research timeline

Every major Google algorithm update, what it targeted, its impact level, and how to recover. This timeline covers 23+ years of Google search changes.

Quick Navigation

2026

June 2026 June 2026 Spam Update High

What changed: Targeted AI-generated spam, scaled content abuse, and link manipulation at unprecedented scale. Integrated new machine learning classifiers for detecting synthetic content patterns.

Recovery: Remove or noindex all AI-generated spam pages. Audit backlink profile for unnatural patterns. Focus on original, human-created content with genuine expertise and author credentials.

March 2026 March 2026 Core Update High

What changed: Broad core algorithm update refining ranking signals for content quality, E-E-A-T evaluation, and user experience metrics. Continued Google's push toward rewarding first-hand experience.

Recovery: Improve content depth and accuracy. Add author bios with demonstrable expertise. Ensure your site provides genuinely helpful, original information that satisfies search intent.

2025

December 2025 December 2025 Core Update High

What changed: Year-end core update with significant shifts in how Google evaluates topical authority and content freshness. Affected YMYL sites heavily.

Recovery: Strengthen topical authority by publishing comprehensive content clusters. Update outdated content with current information. Improve E-E-A-T signals across your site.

November 2025 November 2025 Core Update High

What changed: Major core update that reshuffled rankings across multiple industries. Placed greater emphasis on original reporting and first-hand information.

Recovery: Prioritize original research and reporting. Add unique data, case studies, or experiences to your content. Ensure clear attribution and author expertise signals.

March 2025 March 2025 Core Update High

What changed: First core update of 2025. Refined how Google assesses content helpfulness and site-wide quality signals. Many thin-content sites saw further ranking declines.

Recovery: Audit all pages for quality. Remove or improve thin content. Add comprehensive, well-researched information to every page targeting important keywords.

2024

December 2024 December 2024 Core Update High

What changed: Final core update of 2024. Continued refinement of the integrated Helpful Content System and quality signals. Rolled out over 2 weeks.

Recovery: Focus on creating content that genuinely helps users. Audit and improve pages that don't meet search intent. Build topical authority through comprehensive coverage.

November 2024 November 2024 Core Update High

What changed: Significant core update that continued the push toward rewarding original, helpful content. Many AI-generated content sites saw further ranking drops.

Recovery: Ensure all content provides unique value beyond what AI can generate. Add expert opinions, original data, real-world experience, and actionable insights.

August 2024 August 2024 Core Update High

What changed: Major core update that rolled out over 3 weeks. Significantly impacted sites with thin, unhelpful, or scaled content. One of the largest core updates in impact scope.

Recovery: Conduct a full content audit. Remove or noindex pages that don't serve user needs. Improve pages with genuine expertise. Remove low-quality backlinks.

June 2024 June 2024 Spam Update Medium

What changed: Targeted cloaking, hacked sites, auto-generated spam, and link spam. Applied to multiple languages simultaneously.

Recovery: Ensure your site hasn't been hacked. Remove cloaking and sneaky redirects. Disavow toxic backlinks. Clean up any auto-generated or scraped content.

March 2024 March 2024 Core Update High

What changed: Landmark update that integrated the Helpful Content System directly into the core algorithm. This meant the site-wide content quality signal became a permanent part of core ranking. Also targeted low-quality, mass-produced content.

Recovery: Treat every page as if it must earn its place in search results. Create content with genuine effort, expertise, and value. Remove or significantly improve any low-quality pages.

March 2024 March 2024 Spam Update Medium

What changed: Targeted expired domain abuse, scaled content abuse, and site reputation abuse. Introduced new spam policies alongside the core update.

Recovery: Don't use expired domains for manipulative purposes. Stop publishing large volumes of low-quality content at scale. Ensure third-party content on your site meets quality standards.

2023

October 2023 October 2023 Core Update High

What changed: Broad core update that rolled out over 2 weeks. Continued emphasis on rewarding high-quality, people-first content and penalizing SEO-first content.

Recovery: Create content for humans first, search engines second. Add genuine expertise and unique perspectives. Improve overall site quality and user experience.

August 2023 August 2023 Core Update High

What changed: Significant core update that rolled out over 16 days. Affected rankings broadly across all content types and languages.

Recovery: Focus on content quality over quantity. Ensure every page provides substantial value. Build topical authority with comprehensive content clusters.

March 2023 March 2023 Core Update High

What changed: First core update of 2023. Rolled out over 2 weeks with significant volatility. Refined how Google evaluates overall site quality and content relevance.

Recovery: Improve page-level content quality. Strengthen E-E-A-T signals (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness). Fix technical SEO issues that affect user experience.

February 2023 February 2023 Product Reviews Update Medium

What changed: Continued the Product Reviews update series. Rewarded in-depth, well-researched product reviews with first-hand experience and original research.

Recovery: Write reviews based on actual product testing. Include original photos, pros/cons, comparisons, and alternative recommendations. Update reviews with current information.

2022

September 2022 September 2022 Core Update High

What changed: Core update that rolled out over 2 weeks. Refined ranking systems for content quality assessment and user satisfaction signals.

Recovery: Improve content comprehensiveness and accuracy. Ensure your site provides a good user experience across all devices. Remove or improve underperforming pages.

May 2022 May 2022 Core Update High

What changed: Broad core update that took nearly 2 weeks to roll out. Impacted rankings across multiple industries and languages.

Recovery: Focus on building topical authority. Create comprehensive, well-researched content that fully covers topics. Improve internal linking structure.

March 2022 March 2022 Product Reviews Update Medium

What changed: Third iteration of the Product Reviews update. Refined ability to identify high-quality product review content vs. thin, affiliate-focused reviews.

Recovery: Create genuinely helpful product reviews with first-hand experience. Include detailed comparisons, testing methodology, and honest opinions. Disclose affiliate relationships.

December 2022 December 2022 Helpful Content Update Medium

What changed: Update to the Helpful Content system (originally launched August 2022). Expanded to more languages and refined detection of unhelpful content.

Recovery: Ensure all content is written for people, not search engines. Remove or improve pages created primarily to rank rather than help users. Focus on expertise and value.

August 2022 August 2022 Helpful Content Update High

What changed: Introduced the Helpful Content System as a new ranking signal. Created a site-wide classifier that demotes content written primarily for search engines rather than humans. One of the most significant updates in modern SEO.

Recovery: Create content that demonstrates genuine expertise and helps users accomplish their goals. Remove unhelpful content entirely or rewrite it with substantially more value. Ensure your site has a clear focus and purpose.

2021

December 2021 December 2021 Core Update High

What changed: Final core update of 2021. Continued refining content quality evaluation and user satisfaction signals.

Recovery: Update outdated content. Improve page experience signals. Ensure your content meets search intent better than competing results.

November 2021 November 2021 Local Search Update Medium

What changed: Broad update to the local search ranking system. Refined how Google evaluates local business relevance, prominence, and proximity.

Recovery: Optimize your Google Business Profile completely. Build local citations and reviews. Ensure NAP consistency across the web. Create locally relevant content.

July 2021 July 2021 Link Spam Update Medium

What changed: Multi-week update targeting link spam across multiple languages. Improved ability to detect and nullify unnatural links.

Recovery: Audit and disavow toxic backlinks. Stop participating in link schemes. Focus on earning links through quality content. Use nofollow or sponsored attributes where appropriate.

July 2021 July 2021 Core Update High

What changed: Broad core update that rolled out over 2 weeks. Impacted ranking signals across all content types and industries.

Recovery: Improve overall content quality. Ensure your site provides comprehensive coverage of your topics. Build topical authority through consistent, high-quality publishing.

April 2021 April 2021 Core Update High

What changed: First core update of 2021. Rolled out over 2 weeks with significant ranking changes across multiple SERPs.

Recovery: Focus on creating the best content for your target keywords. Improve E-E-A-T signals. Ensure your site meets Core Web Vitals thresholds.

April 2021 April 2021 Product Reviews Update Medium

What changed: First Product Reviews update. Rewarded product reviews with in-depth research, original analysis, and genuine expertise over superficial reviews.

Recovery: Write detailed product reviews based on actual hands-on testing. Include original images, video, and comparisons. Provide pros, cons, and alternative recommendations.

February 2021 February 2021 Passage Ranking Medium

What changed: Google began using passage-based indexing to rank specific passages within a page independently. This allowed deep, specific content within long pages to surface for relevant queries.

Recovery: Structure content with clear headings and focused sections. Use descriptive H2/H3 tags. Ensure each passage within a page provides standalone value for its topic.

2020

December 2020 December 2020 Core Update High

What changed: Final core update of 2020. Significant ranking volatility across many industries. Continued emphasis on content quality and E-E-A-T signals.

Recovery: Improve content depth and expertise. Update outdated information. Strengthen author credentials and site authority signals.

May 2020 May 2020 Core Update High

What changed: One of the largest core updates ever. Rolled out over 2 weeks with major ranking shifts across virtually all industries and regions.

Recovery: Conduct a comprehensive content audit. Remove or improve thin content. Ensure your site provides genuine value and expertise in your niche.

January 2020 January 2020 Core Update Medium

What changed: First core update of 2020. Broad impact on rankings across many categories, though less severe than the May update.

Recovery: Focus on content quality, comprehensiveness, and user experience. Ensure your site meets Core Web Vitals requirements.

2019

October 2019 BERT High

What changed: Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers — the most significant change to Google's algorithm in 5 years. BERT improved Google's ability to understand natural language and search intent, particularly for conversational and ambiguous queries. Affected 10% of all searches at launch.

Recovery: Write content in natural, conversational language. Focus on answering specific questions thoroughly. Optimize for search intent rather than keyword matching.

June 2019 June 2019 Core Update Medium

What changed: Broad core update. Google confirmed it was a general improvement to how they assess content quality and relevance.

Recovery: Create comprehensive, authoritative content. Improve E-E-A-T signals. Ensure your site covers topics thoroughly.

March 2019 March 2019 Core Update Medium

What changed: Confirmed broad core update that rolled out over 3 days. Impacted rankings across multiple SERPs.

Recovery: Focus on creating high-quality, original content. Improve your site's overall authority and trustworthiness.

May 2019 Deindexing Bug Low

What changed: A bug caused Google to accidentally deindex some pages from search results. Google fixed the issue within days.

Recovery: If affected, pages were automatically re-indexed after the fix. No action required from webmasters.

2018

August 2018 August 2018 Core Update ("Medic") High

What changed: Widely called the "Medic" update because it heavily impacted health, medical, and YMYL (Your Money Your Life) sites. Significantly increased the weight of E-A-T signals for pages that could affect a person's health, financial stability, or safety. One of the most impactful core updates in history.

Recovery: For YMYL content, demonstrate clear expertise with credentialed authors, citations to authoritative sources, and medically/legally accurate information. Build site-wide trust signals.

July 2018 Chrome Security (HTTPS) Medium

What changed: Chrome began marking HTTP sites as "Not Secure." While not a direct algorithm update, the shift to HTTPS as a ranking signal was reinforced, and user trust in non-HTTPS sites declined.

Recovery: Migrate your site to HTTPS. Implement proper redirects from HTTP to HTTPS. Ensure all resources load over HTTPS to avoid mixed content warnings.

March 2018 March 2018 Core Update High

What changed: Broad core update that significantly impacted rankings. Google confirmed it targeted relevance and quality signals.

Recovery: Improve content comprehensiveness and accuracy. Ensure your pages satisfy user intent better than competing results.

2017

March 2017 Fred Medium

What changed: Named "Fred" by SEOs, this update targeted low-quality content created primarily for ad revenue rather than user value. Affected sites with thin affiliate content, aggressive ads, and low-quality doorway pages.

Recovery: Reduce ad density and improve content quality. Remove thin affiliate pages. Ensure every page provides genuine value beyond monetization.

January 2017 Intrusive Interstitials Penalty Medium

What changed: Google began penalizing pages with intrusive interstitials (pop-ups) that made content difficult to access, particularly on mobile devices.

Recovery: Remove or reduce intrusive pop-ups on mobile. Use banner-style notifications instead of full-screen overlays. Ensure content is easily accessible without dismissal.

2016

September 2016 Penguin 4.0 High

What changed: Penguin was integrated into Google's core algorithm as a real-time signal. Instead of penalizing sites, it now devalues bad links. It operates in real-time, meaning link cleanup has faster effect. This was the final major Penguin update.

Recovery: Audit and disavow toxic backlinks. Focus on earning natural links through quality content. Remove or nofollow paid or manipulative links. Penguin now devalues rather than penalizes.

September 2016 Possum Medium

What changed: Major local search update. Businesses outside city limits got more visibility. Also affected results based on searcher's location and the address of the business.

Recovery: Optimize Google Business Profile. Build local citations. Ensure NAP consistency. Get genuine local reviews.

2015

October 2015 RankBrain High

What changed: Google's first major machine learning-based ranking system. RankBrain helps process and understand search queries, particularly ambiguous or never-before-seen queries. Became the third most important ranking factor. Marked Google's shift from purely algorithmic to AI-assisted ranking.

Recovery: Create content that satisfies search intent comprehensively. Write naturally and address the full scope of user questions. Focus on topic coverage rather than exact keyword matching.

April 2015 Mobilegeddon (Mobile-Friendly Update) High

What changed: Google began using mobile-friendliness as a ranking signal. Pages not optimized for mobile lost ranking in mobile search results. Nicknamed "Mobilegeddon" though the impact was more gradual than feared.

Recovery: Make your site responsive with mobile-friendly design. Ensure tap targets are large enough. Use legible font sizes. Avoid Flash and use modern web technologies.

2014

July 2014 Pigeon Medium

What changed: Major local search algorithm update that improved distance and location ranking parameters. Created closer ties between local and organic ranking signals. Significantly changed local pack results.

Recovery: Optimize for local search with accurate NAP information. Build local citations and backlinks. Ensure Google Business Profile is complete and accurate. Create locally relevant content.

August 2014 HTTPS/SSL Boost Low

What changed: Google announced HTTPS as a lightweight ranking signal. Sites using SSL/TLS encryption received a small ranking boost.

Recovery: Migrate your site to HTTPS. It's a lightweight signal but has become increasingly important over time.

2013

August 2013 Hummingbird High

What changed: A complete overhaul of Google's core search algorithm — the biggest change since 2001. Hummingbird improved Google's ability to understand the meaning behind queries, not just keywords. Enabled better handling of conversational search and long-tail queries.

Recovery: Create comprehensive content that answers user questions naturally. Focus on topics and entities rather than just keywords. Write for conversational search queries.

June 2013 Payday Loan 2.0 Low

What changed: Targeted spammy queries in industries like payday loans, gambling, and other highly spammed niches.

Recovery: For sites in affected industries, focus on building genuine authority and creating legitimate, helpful content. Avoid spammy link building tactics.

2012

May 2012 Penguin 2.0 High

What changed: Fourth iteration of the Penguin algorithm. Deeper integration into Google's core ranking signals. Better at detecting link spam and manipulative link building practices.

Recovery: Clean up your backlink profile. Remove or disavow toxic links. Stop participating in link exchanges, paid links, and other manipulative link schemes.

August 2012 DMCA Penalty (Pirate Update) Medium

What changed: Targeted sites that repeatedly received DMCA takedown notices for copyright infringement. Sites with many piracy-related complaints saw ranking drops.

Recovery: Ensure your site doesn't host or link to pirated content. Respond to DMCA takedown notices promptly. Create original content.

September 2012 Exact Match Domain (EMD) Update Medium

What changed: Reduced rankings for low-quality sites with exact-match domain names. Prevented sites from ranking well simply because their domain matched a search query.

Recovery: Don't rely on domain name alone for rankings. Build genuine content quality and authority. Focus on creating valuable, comprehensive content.

2011

February 2011 Panda High

What changed: One of the most transformative updates in Google history. Panda targeted low-quality, thin content farms and sites with high ad-to-content ratios. Initially affected ~12% of all searches. Created a site-wide quality score that affected all pages. Fundamentally changed how Google evaluated content quality.

Recovery: Remove or significantly improve thin, low-quality content. Reduce ad density. Increase content depth and originality. Add expert authorship signals. Ensure a positive user experience.

November 2011 Freshness Update Medium

What changed: Improved Google's ability to identify and rank fresh, timely content for time-sensitive queries. Affected ~35% of searches.

Recovery: Keep content updated and current. Publish fresh content regularly for time-sensitive topics. Update publication dates when making significant content changes.

June 2011 Schema.org Low

What changed: Google, Bing, and Yahoo jointly announced Schema.org for structured data markup. While not an algorithm update per se, it changed how search engines understood page content and enabled rich results.

Recovery: Implement Schema.org structured data markup on your pages. Use appropriate schema types for your content (Article, Product, FAQ, etc.).

2010

June 2010 Caffeine High

What changed: Complete rebuild of Google's indexing system. Caffeine enabled 50% fresher results and could index the web faster than the previous system. It was a foundational infrastructure change that enabled faster crawling and indexing of new content.

Recovery: Ensure your site is crawlable and indexable. Submit XML sitemaps. Optimize server response times. Create fresh, regularly updated content.

September 2010 Instant Search Low

What changed: Google Instant showed search results as users typed. Changed user behavior around search and how queries were constructed.

Recovery: No direct SEO action needed. Focus on ranking for relevant keywords and satisfying search intent.

2009

December 2009 Real-time Search Low

What changed: Google added real-time results to search, showing recent social media posts and news articles as they were published.

Recovery: Publish timely, relevant content for trending topics. Maintain active social media presence for real-time visibility.

2008

February 2008 Vince/Brand Update Medium

What changed: Gave preference to established brands in search results. Small sites saw ranking declines while big brands gained visibility. Often interpreted as Google favoring authority and trust signals.

Recovery: Build brand authority through consistent, high-quality content and PR. Develop trust signals and demonstrate expertise in your niche.

2007

June 2007 Buffy Low

What changed: Minor update that improved how Google handled localized search results and map integration. Named by SEOs, not officially confirmed by Google.

Recovery: Ensure accurate local business information. Optimize for local search with proper citations and listings.

2006

2006 Supplemental Index Update Low

What changed: Changes to Google's supplemental index affected how lower-quality pages were crawled and ranked. Pages in the supplemental index received less crawling priority.

Recovery: Improve page quality to move out of the supplemental index. Build internal links to important pages. Ensure unique, valuable content on every page.

2005

October 2005 Jagger Medium

What changed: Series of updates targeting reciprocal link exchanges, link farms, and paid links. Improved Google's ability to detect manipulative link building.

Recovery: Stop participating in link exchanges and paid link schemes. Focus on earning natural links through quality content.

2005 Google Local/Maps Low

What changed: Launch of Google Maps integration in search results, creating local business listings and map-based search results.

Recovery: Claim and optimize your Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business listing).

2005 Sitelinks Search Box Low

What changed: Added a search box to sitelinks for branded queries, allowing users to search within a site directly from Google results.

Recovery: Implement internal search functionality. Ensure your site structure allows easy navigation. Submit a sitemap to help Google understand your site structure.

2004

February 2004 Allegra Medium

What changed: Affected sites with "suspicious" link profiles. Targeted link buying, link networks, and other manipulative linking practices.

Recovery: Build natural backlinks through quality content. Avoid paid links and link networks.

January 2004 Austin Medium

What changed: Continued the Florida update's impact, targeting invisible text and keyword stuffing more aggressively.

Recovery: Write naturally for humans. Avoid keyword stuffing, hidden text, and other on-page spam tactics.

2003

November 2003 Florida High

What changed: The update that changed SEO forever. Florida was the first major algorithm update to receive widespread attention. It decimated sites using keyword stuffing, hidden text, and other on-page spam tactics. Forced the SEO industry to shift from manipulation to genuine quality.

Recovery: Remove all keyword stuffing, hidden text, and spam tactics. Create content for users, not search engines. Focus on genuine quality and relevance.

June 2003 Cassandra Low

What changed: Targeted link farms and sites with duplicate content. Improved detection of non-unique content and manipulative linking.

Recovery: Create original content. Avoid link farms and duplicate content across multiple pages.

May 2003 Esmeralda Low

What changed: Primarily affected how Google ranked pages with similar content. Improved duplicate content detection.

Recovery: Ensure each page provides unique value. Avoid duplicate content across your site.

February 2003 Boston Medium

What changed: First named Google update. Targeted "white hat" SEO techniques that Google considered over-optimized, including excessive keyword density and on-page manipulation.

Recovery: Use natural keyword usage. Focus on creating genuinely helpful content rather than over-optimizing for search engines.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often does Google update its algorithm?

Google makes thousands of changes to its search algorithm every year. Most are minor and go unnoticed, but Google confirmed 4,500+ changes in 2024 alone. Major core updates typically occur 2-4 times per year, while spam updates and specific system updates happen on a rolling basis.

What is a Google core update?

A Google core update is a broad change to the search algorithm and systems that affects how content is ranked across all languages and regions. Core updates are released several times per year and can significantly shift rankings for many websites. Unlike targeted updates, core updates affect the entire ranking system rather than a specific signal.

How long does it take to recover from a Google algorithm update?

Recovery time varies depending on the update and changes made. After a core update, sites may see ranking changes within days to weeks, but meaningful recovery typically takes 3-6 months. Recovery requires making substantive improvements to content quality, not just waiting. Google must re-crawl and re-evaluate your pages.

What was the biggest Google algorithm update in history?

The most impactful Google algorithm updates include Panda (2011), which devalued thin content across the web; Penguin (2012), which targeted manipulative link building; and the Helpful Content Update (2022), which created a site-wide signal rewarding people-first content. The March 2024 Core Update was also significant as it integrated the Helpful Content System directly into the core algorithm.

How do I check if a Google algorithm update affected my site?

Monitor your Google Search Console Performance report for sudden drops in impressions or clicks. Check Google's Search Status Dashboard for confirmed updates. Compare ranking changes against known update dates using tools like SEMrush Sensor, Moz Cast, or Ahrefs Rank Tracker. A drop correlating with a confirmed update date indicates the update affected your site.
